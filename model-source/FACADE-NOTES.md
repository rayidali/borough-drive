## Confirmed visual counts and geometry

- **SW 159–161: six total stories**, retail plus five residential. Gray/buff brick; a continuous projecting rust-red/brown cornice with brackets and repeated swag ornament wraps both street walls. The clearly visible upper First Avenue rows have **7 individual sash windows grouped 2 / 1 / 2 / 2**, plus a blind decorative recess toward the intersection corner. Top two window rows use splayed pale lintels; middle rows have pediment/lintel ornament. Black zigzag fire escapes are present on both walls. Trees obstruct the lower First Avenue rows. The north-facing wall is oblique and partly obscured by fire escapes in the available photos; an exact complete window count there is **not established**, so do not infer it from the First Avenue count. Best: `sw-159-first-2021-09-06.jpg` (CC BY 2.0; 3024×4032). The 2021 ground-floor corner has an eyes/health optical sign; Pinks is on the East 10th side. These are historical storefront states.

- **NW 163: three total stories**, retail plus two residential. First Avenue has **2 broad tripartite window openings per upper floor**, each with a wide center light and narrow side lights. The upper East 10th row has **8 opening positions**: seven broad groups and one narrow single nearest First Avenue; trees obscure several lower-row windows. Red/orange stucco or painted render, with horizontal scoring on the lower residential floor; narrow green belt courses and green parapet/cornice trim. The storefront base is cream with brown awnings. One red fire-escape/roof ladder assembly sits partway down the East 10th side; no fire escape crosses the two First Avenue window groups in this view. Best complete geometry source: `nw-163-167-jll-full-2026-listing-unknown-photo-date.jpg` (3098×2153, **reuse license unknown**). Photo date unknown; it shows Tarallucci, which closed June 2022. No similarly complete openly licensed NW corner photo was located in this bounded search. The two CC BY Tarallucci photos mainly show the sidewalk/storefront and are poor upper-wall references.

- **SE 162: four total stories**, retail plus three residential. First Avenue has **3 single sash openings per residential row**, red brick, modest rectangular lintels/sills and a shallow brick/stone parapet rather than an ornate projecting roof cornice. A red fire escape spans the First Avenue frontage. The East 10th wall includes conspicuous bricked-in window positions and broad plain brick fields; the complete wall's exact open/blocked count is not established in the selected view. `First_and_10th_(51645797940).jpg` gives the full First Avenue facade and the tall ornate adjacent 160 First facade. **Honor EXIF orientation 6**: encoded 4032×3024, displayed 3024×4032. Both this photo and `E_10th_Street_Finest_Deli_(52057926354).jpg` are CC BY 2.0. Deli signage/graffiti represents 2021–2022, not verified present-day occupancy.

- **NE 164: four total street-facing stories**, retail plus three window rows. First Avenue has **3 single windows per row**. Red brick; pale gray/green lintels; a restrained projecting gray/green cornice; roof-mounted white cellular antenna panels on a metal rack. The lowest residential row is painted charcoal and has two vertical Japanese-art panels between windows. The clear main East 10th wall has **3 open windows plus 1 bricked-up opening on each of its upper two residential rows**; the open windows are regular / narrow / regular. The lowest residential row has **4 open windows** on that main plane, with a large mural between the first two. A black fire escape crosses the eastern group, and bundled black cables descend from the roof. A narrow continuation at the 245 doorway and a large pale exhaust duct are visible at the east end; use the photo to preserve this irregularity. Best licensed wall references: `ne-e10th-side-2022-05-26-1920.jpg`, `ne-first-front-2022-05-26-1920.jpg` (Kidfly182, CC BY-SA 4.0). `ne-beron-beron-2025-05-16.jpg` is a CC BY 2.0 original with excellent window/art/lantern detail. The JLL front photo is a strong orthogonal geometry reference, but its reuse license is unknown.

## Adjacent geometry that should remain separate

- The **247 East 10th building is taller than the 164 corner mass**, with five total stories, a much more ornate dark cornice, red brick, projecting dark lintels and a central greenish fire escape. The official/real-estate descriptions calling the shared property “five stories” must not be applied uniformly to the four-story corner. `ne-247-e10th-adjacent-2022-05-26-1920.jpg` clearly shows the 245 door, duct and the taller 247 facade. Trees obscure its far eastern portion.
- The **New Theatre Building / 240 East 10th tower** rises behind the SW corner over a much lower street base. `new-theatre-building-2021-06-06-1920.jpg` shows the complete east-facing tower elevation, projecting balcony stack, mechanical vents below horizontal window groups, and the low theater base. The SW full-corner photo additionally shows the tower's north-side changes of plane and stepped roof/massing. Both are CC BY 2.0 and date from 2021. Do not extrude the tower height across the entire low base footprint.
